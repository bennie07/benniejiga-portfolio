Phishing Overview

ID: 1.2

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of social engineering attack that attempts to steal sensitive information by disguising as a trustworthy entity.

Key Facts:

  • 90% of data breaches involve phishing
  • Costs organizations an average of $4.65 million per breach
  • Increasingly sophisticated and targeted
  • Everyone is a potential target

Email Phishing

Mass email campaigns designed to steal credentials or deliver malware. Often contain urgent messages, suspicious links, or attachments.

  • Most common type of phishing
  • Uses mass email campaigns
  • Creates artificial urgency
  • Often contains suspicious links or attachments

Spear Phishing

Targeted attacks aimed at specific individuals or organizations. Customized with personal information to increase credibility.

  • Highly targeted to specific individuals
  • Uses personal information to build trust
  • Often researches victims on social media
  • Higher success rate than generic phishing

Smishing (SMS Phishing)

Phishing attempts delivered via text messages. Often contain shortened URLs and urgent requests.

  • Delivered through text/SMS messages
  • Often contains shortened URLs
  • Exploits mobile device limitations
  • Commonly impersonates banks, delivery services

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Phone-based phishing that uses social engineering to obtain sensitive information or install malware through callback requests.

  • Uses phone calls to extract information
  • Often impersonates technical support
  • Creates scenarios requiring immediate action
  • May direct victims to malicious websites

Response Protocol Flowchart

ID: 1.6

Phishing Response Protocol

Follow these steps when you encounter a suspicious email.

1. Identify Suspicious Communication

Look for common phishing indicators such as unexpected emails, urgent language, suspicious links, etc.

Decision Point

"Is the sender verified AND content expected?"

Yes
Proceed with normal action
No / Unsure

2. Do Not Click Links or Open Attachments

Clicking links or attachments in phishing emails may install malware or compromise credentials.

3. Do Not Reply or Forward (except to security)

Don't engage with the sender or forward to colleagues unless specifically instructed by security.

4. Report to Security Team

Use the approved reporting method to alert the security team about the suspicious communication.

5. Delete Email After Confirmation

Only delete the suspicious email after receiving confirmation from the security team.
