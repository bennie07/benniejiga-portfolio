Subject Matter Expert Interview Guide

Framework for Effective Knowledge Extraction

Designer: Bennie Ejiga
Date: March 28, 2025
Version: 1.0

Purpose of This Guide

This comprehensive interview guide provides a structured framework for extracting critical knowledge from Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) during the instructional design process. It serves to:

  • Optimize limited SME time through structured, purposeful interactions
  • Ensure comprehensive content coverage aligned with learning objectives
  • Extract both explicit and tacit knowledge from experts
  • Document critical information in a consistent, usable format
  • Establish productive collaborative relationships with SMEs
  • Transform specialized expertise into learner-appropriate content

This guide can be customized for different content domains, project types, and SME availability constraints.

Interview Structure Framework

Opening Phase (5-10 minutes)

Step Purpose Sample Script/Questions
Welcome and appreciation Establish positive rapport "Thank you for sharing your expertise with us. Your knowledge is critical to developing effective training."
Project overview refresh Ensure shared understanding of purpose "As a quick reminder, we're developing training for [audience] to help them [performance goal]."
Set expectations for session Create structure and clarity "Today we'll focus specifically on [topic areas]. We have [time] scheduled, and I'll be guiding us through specific questions."
Explain knowledge capture process Ensure comfort with methods "I'll be taking notes and recording our session to ensure I capture your expertise accurately. Everything will be shared with you for review."
Address any initial questions Resolve concerns "Do you have any questions about our process or what we're trying to accomplish before we begin?"

Core Content Extraction (30-60 minutes)

Content Category Purpose Sample Questions
Conceptual Foundation Establish fundamental knowledge framework
  • "What are the most essential concepts someone needs to understand about [topic]?"
  • "How would you explain [concept] to someone completely new to this field?"
  • "What are common misconceptions about [topic] that we should address?"
Procedural Knowledge Document processes and workflows
  • "What are the specific steps involved in [process]?"
  • "What decision points occur during this process?"
  • "How do you know when each step is completed correctly?"
  • "What variations exist in how this procedure might be performed?"
Contextual Application Understand real-world implementation
  • "In what situations would someone need to apply this knowledge?"
  • "What environmental factors affect how this is implemented?"
  • "How does the application of this change based on [variables]?"
  • "What real-world constraints impact this process?"
Critical Thinking/Decision Making Extract expert judgment processes
  • "How do you determine which approach to use when [situation]?"
  • "What factors do you consider when making decisions about [topic]?"
  • "What subtle cues or indicators influence your choices?"
  • "Walk me through your thought process for a challenging scenario."
Troubleshooting/Problem Solving Capture diagnostic expertise
  • "What are the most common problems encountered with [topic/process]?"
  • "How do you identify the root cause of [problem]?"
  • "What solutions work best for [specific issue]?"
  • "What preventative measures should be taken?"

Scenario and Example Elicitation (15-20 minutes)

Example Type Purpose Elicitation Techniques
Success Cases Illustrate ideal application
  • "Can you describe a situation where you've seen this done exceptionally well?"
  • "What specific actions or decisions led to success in that case?"
  • "What measurable outcomes demonstrated success?"
Failure Examples Highlight common pitfalls
  • "What are typical mistakes people make when learning this?"
  • "Can you share an anonymized example of when this went wrong?"
  • "What early warning signs might indicate potential problems?"
Edge Cases Explore boundary conditions
  • "What unusual situations might require adapting the standard approach?"
  • "How do you handle exceptions to the normal process?"
  • "What rare but important scenarios should learners be aware of?"
Before/After Comparisons Show transformation
  • "How does performance typically change after mastering this skill?"
  • "What specific differences would you observe between a novice and expert approach?"
  • "How have you seen individuals or teams transform after learning this?"
Visual Examples Capture visual knowledge
  • "Could you sketch/diagram how this looks when done correctly?"
  • "What visual cues indicate [condition/state]?"
  • "Are there existing visual examples we could reference?"

Performance Standards Clarification (10-15 minutes)

Standard Component Purpose Sample Questions
Success Criteria Define acceptable performance
  • "How do you know when someone has performed this task correctly?"
  • "What specific standards must be met?"
  • "What measurable outcomes indicate competence?"
Evaluation Methods Establish assessment approach
  • "How is proficiency currently evaluated?"
  • "What performance indicators are most meaningful?"
  • "What testing methods would authentically assess capability?"
Common Errors Identify critical mistakes
  • "What errors are most frequent among beginners?"
  • "Which mistakes have the most serious consequences?"
  • "What near-misses might appear correct but aren't?"
Proficiency Levels Differentiate expertise stages
  • "How would you distinguish between basic, intermediate, and advanced performance?"
  • "What capabilities differentiate an expert from a competent performer?"
  • "What developmental milestones indicate progress?"
Constraints and Conditions Clarify performance context
  • "Under what conditions must this performance occur?"
  • "What time, accuracy, or resource constraints apply?"
  • "What environmental factors affect acceptable performance?"

Resources and Tools Identification (5-10 minutes)

Resource Type Purpose Sample Questions
Reference Materials Identify supporting documentation
  • "What reference materials do you consult regularly?"
  • "Are there manuals, guides, or standards we should incorporate?"
  • "What documentation would be helpful for learners?"
Tools and Technology Capture required implements
  • "What specific tools or technologies are used in this process?"
  • "Are there preferred tools vs. acceptable alternatives?"
  • "What technology limitations might learners encounter?"
Job Aids Identify performance support
  • "What checklists or quick references would support this task?"
  • "Are there existing job aids we could adapt?"
  • "What information is most critical during performance?"
Practice Opportunities Identify skill development methods
  • "How do people typically practice and develop this skill?"
  • "What safe environments exist for experimentation?"
  • "What simulations or scenarios would be valuable?"
Expert Networks Map human resources
  • "Who else has expertise in this area?"
  • "Where do practitioners go for help or advice?"
  • "What communities of practice exist around this topic?"

Closing and Next Steps (5-10 minutes)

Step Purpose Sample Script/Questions
Summary review Confirm understanding "To summarize what we've covered today: [key points]. Have I captured your insights accurately?"
Gap identification Identify missing information "What important aspects of this topic haven't we addressed yet?"
Follow-up planning Establish next interactions "I'd like to schedule our next session to focus on [topics]. How does [date/time] work for you?"
Content review expectations Set review process "I'll send you notes and draft content from our session by [date]. Could you review them by [deadline]?"
Express appreciation Maintain relationship "Your expertise has been incredibly valuable. Thank you for your time and insights today."

Specialized Questioning Techniques

Knowledge Elicitation Methods

Technique When to Use Process Example
Critical Incident Technique To extract tacit knowledge from significant experiences Ask SME to recall specific incidents that were particularly challenging or illustrative "Can you tell me about a time when you faced an unusual challenge with this system? What happened, what did you do, and what was the outcome?"
Cognitive Task Analysis To understand expert thought processes Have SME think aloud while solving problems or making decisions "As you work through this example, please verbalize your thinking process—what you're noticing, considering, and deciding at each step."
Concept Mapping To visualize knowledge structure and relationships Collaboratively create diagram showing concepts and their connections "Let's create a map showing how these concepts relate to each other. What would be the central concept, and what connects directly to it?"
Repertory Grid Technique To identify distinctions experts make that novices miss Present examples and ask how some are similar but different from others "Looking at these three scenarios, how are two of them similar but different from the third in terms of how you would approach them?"
Process Tracing To capture detailed procedural knowledge Have SME walk through process while you document each step and decision point "Please walk me through this process from start to finish, highlighting each step, decision point, and what you're looking for along the way."

Probing Questions for Deeper Insights

Question Type Purpose Example Questions
Clarification Probes Ensure understanding of terminology and concepts
  • "When you say [term], could you define exactly what that means?"
  • "How would you explain [concept] to someone new to the field?"
  • "Could you give an example of what you mean by [term]?"
Elaboration Probes Expand on incomplete information
  • "Could you tell me more about [aspect]?"
  • "What else should I know about this that we haven't discussed?"
  • "Could you expand on why that approach is effective?"
Contrast Probes Identify boundaries and distinctions
  • "How is [approach A] different from [approach B]?"
  • "What distinguishes an effective versus ineffective approach here?"
  • "How does this process change under different conditions?"
Process Probes Uncover steps and sequences
  • "What happens before this step?"
  • "What comes next in the process?"
  • "Are there any preparatory steps that need to happen?"
Rationale Probes Understand underlying reasoning
  • "Why is this step necessary?"
  • "What's the purpose behind this requirement?"
  • "How did this best practice develop?"
Consequence Probes Explore impacts and outcomes
  • "What happens if this step is performed incorrectly?"
  • "How does this decision affect downstream processes?"
  • "What are the long-term implications of this approach?"
Exception Probes Identify boundaries and special cases
  • "When would you NOT use this approach?"
  • "Are there situations where the standard process doesn't apply?"
  • "What exceptions have you encountered?"

Core Knowledge Capture Templates

Concept Definition Template

Element Description Example
Concept Name Official terminology Multifactor Authentication (MFA)
Simple Definition Learner-friendly explanation A security process requiring users to verify their identity in two or more ways before gaining access
Importance/Relevance Why it matters to learners Critical defense against unauthorized access even when passwords are compromised
Key Characteristics Essential attributes
  • Requires multiple verification types
  • Typically combines knowledge, possession, and inherence factors
  • Provides layered security approach
Examples Concrete instances
  • Password + smartphone verification code
  • Password + fingerprint scan
  • Password + security token
Non-Examples What it is not
  • Using only a password (single factor)
  • Two passwords (same factor type)
  • Security questions (still knowledge factor)
Related Concepts Connected ideas
  • Password policies
  • Security tokens
  • Biometric authentication
Common Misconceptions Typical misunderstandings
  • Myth: MFA is inconvenient and slows productivity
  • Myth: MFA is infallible and cannot be bypassed

Procedure Documentation Template

Element Description Example
Procedure Name Official process title Account Lockout Resolution Procedure
Purpose Why this procedure exists To securely restore access to locked user accounts while maintaining security protocols
When to Use Triggering conditions When a user reports inability to log in due to multiple failed password attempts
Prerequisites Required conditions/resources
  • User identity verification process
  • Account management system access
  • Authorization to modify account status
Step-by-Step Process Detailed procedure steps
  1. Verify user identity using approved verification protocol
  2. Access account management system
  3. Locate user account using employee ID
  4. Check account status and confirm lockout
  5. Document verification method used
  6. Reset account status to active
  7. Request user to create new password
  8. Document incident in security log
Decision Points Choices during process
  • If identity cannot be verified: Escalate to supervisor
  • If lockout is suspicious: Initiate security investigation protocol
  • If multiple lockouts exist: Review for potential security incident
Completion Criteria How to know it's done correctly
  • User can successfully log in
  • New password established
  • Incident properly documented
  • No security alerts triggered
Common Issues Typical problems
  • User fails secondary verification
  • System shows different lockout cause
  • User immediate lockout recurrence
Troubleshooting Solutions to problems
  • For verification failure: Use alternate verification method
  • For system discrepancy: Cross-check with security logs
  • For repeated lockouts: Check for keystroke issues or malware

Challenging SME Situations

Common Challenges and Solutions

Challenge Possible Causes Solutions
Highly Technical SME
  • Expertise gap between SME and ID
  • SME used to technical audiences
  • Assumption of background knowledge
  • Do advance research on basic concepts
  • Ask for explanations of technical terms
  • Use "explain it to a novice" technique
  • Focus on application before technical details
Limited Availability
  • SME time constraints
  • Competing priorities
  • Insufficient project allocation
  • Thoroughly prepare to maximize session time
  • Break into shorter, focused sessions
  • Provide questions in advance
  • Consider asynchronous methods (surveys, recordings)
Difficulty Articulating Knowledge
  • Tacit knowledge challenges
  • Limited teaching experience
  • Lack of self-awareness about expertise
  • Use scenario-based questions
  • Apply cognitive task analysis techniques
  • Use visual mapping and comparisons
  • Ask for examples rather than explanations
Resistance to Simplification
  • Concern about oversimplification
  • Pride in technical complexity
  • Fear of dumbing down content
  • Emphasize learner needs and context
  • Suggest layered approach (basics → complexity)
  • Use concept of "progressive disclosure"
  • Assure technical details will be preserved where needed
Content Scope Expansion
  • Enthusiasm for subject matter
  • Difficulty prioritizing content
  • Desire to include everything
  • Continually reference learning objectives
  • Use prioritization exercises
  • Create "parking lot" for future content
  • Discuss constraints (time, cognitive load)

Advanced Facilitation Techniques for Challenging Situations

Situation Technique Implementation
SME Contradicts Earlier Information Constructive reconciliation
  • Note the discrepancy without judgment
  • Ask for clarification on apparent contradiction
  • Explore contextual differences that explain both perspectives
  • Document the nuance and conditions for each approach
Multiple SMEs Disagree Productive resolution
  • Acknowledge different valid approaches may exist
  • Ask each SME to explain their reasoning
  • Focus on contexts where each approach is optimal
  • Document decision criteria for learners where appropriate
SME Reluctant to Share "Secret Sauce" Trust building
  • Acknowledge proprietary nature of expertise
  • Clarify how information will be used and protected
  • Discuss benefits of knowledge transfer
  • Offer review rights and attribution
SME Overwhelms with Information Strategic focusing
  • Use timeboxing for topics
  • Create progressive topic lists
  • Employ "top three" prioritization technique
  • Use parking lot for tangential information
SME Has Limited Expression Skills Structured extraction
  • Provide visual frameworks and templates
  • Use direct observation of performance
  • Offer multiple-choice options rather than open questions
  • Use examples and ask for comparison/critique

Interview Documentation Best Practices

Recording and Note-Taking Framework

Documentation Type Purpose Best Practices
Interview Notes Capture key points during session
  • Use standardized templates with predefined sections
  • Focus on critical information rather than transcription
  • Note SME emphasis, concerns, and uncertainties
  • Mark items requiring clarification or follow-up
Audio/Video Recording Preserve exact information for later reference
  • Always obtain permission before recording
  • Test recording setup before beginning
  • Use backup recording method when possible
  • Inform SME when recording starts and stops
Process Documentation Capture workflows and procedures
  • Use numbered steps for sequences
  • Document decision points and variables
  • Note exceptions and alternative approaches
  • Validate accuracy with SME during session
Visual Documentation Capture diagrams, models, and examples
  • Use digital whiteboard or drawing tools
  • Take screenshots of SME-created visuals
  • Ask SME to verify visual accuracy
  • Label all components clearly
Follow-up Summary Confirm understanding and next steps
  • Send within 24 hours of interview
  • Highlight key insights and discoveries
  • List specific follow-up questions
  • Include clear action items with deadlines
