Comprehensive Learning Objectives Framework Using Bloom's Taxonomy

Introduction

This framework provides a structured approach to writing clear, measurable learning objectives aligned with Bloom's Taxonomy. Each objective follows the format:

"By the end of this [learning experience], learners will be able to [action verb] [specific content/context] [measurable criteria]."

Using SMART Criteria for Learning Objectives

SMART Element Definition Example in Learning Objectives Poor Example Improved Example
Specific Clearly defines what learners will be able to do "...will be able to create a project budget using Excel templates..." "...will understand budgeting..." "...will be able to create a project budget using the company's Excel template that includes all 5 required cost categories..."
Measurable Includes criteria for measuring achievement "...will respond to customer complaints with 90% satisfaction rating..." "...will improve customer service..." "...will respond to customer complaints using the LAST method, achieving a minimum customer satisfaction rating of 90% on follow-up surveys..."
Achievable Realistic within the scope of training "...will implement 3 new time management techniques..." "...will never miss deadlines again..." "...will implement at least 3 new time management techniques from the training, resulting in meeting deadlines for 90% of assigned tasks..."
Relevant Aligned with job requirements and business needs "...will apply conflict resolution steps to resolve team disagreements..." "...will learn about conflicts..." "...will apply the 5-step conflict resolution process to successfully address team disagreements, as measured by a 30% reduction in escalated issues..."
Time-bound Indicates when the objective should be accomplished "...within the first week after training, will correctly process..." "...will eventually process..." "...within the first week after training, will correctly process customer returns with zero errors on all documentation..."

Example of a Complete Learning Objective Set for a Module

Module: "Effective Customer Communication in Retail Banking"

Knowledge Objectives

  1. By the end of this module, learners will be able to identify the 8 key regulatory requirements that impact customer communications in banking with 100% accuracy on a compliance assessment.
  2. By the end of this module, learners will be able to explain the bank's customer communication protocol in their own words, correctly describing each of the 5 communication steps.
  3. By the end of this module, learners will be able to differentiate between at least 4 types of difficult customer situations and identify the appropriate response protocol for each.

Skill Objectives

  1. By the end of this module, learners will be able to demonstrate active listening techniques in at least 3 different customer scenarios, incorporating all required elements from the communication checklist.
  2. By the end of this module, learners will be able to de-escalate an upset customer using the CALM framework, successfully reducing tension in at least 4 out of 5 role-play scenarios.
  3. By the end of this module, learners will be able to communicate complex financial information using appropriate plain language, with 90% customer comprehension as measured in simulation exercises.

Attitude Objectives

  1. By the end of this module, learners will demonstrate a customer-first mindset by prioritizing customer needs in their decision-making during scenario-based assessments.
  2. By the end of this module, learners will value diverse communication styles by adapting their approach to different customer preferences in at least 3 interaction types.

Application Objectives

  1. Within 30 days of completing this module, learners will apply at least 3 new communication techniques on the job, as documented in their application journal.
  2. Within 60 days of completing this module, learners will demonstrate a 20% improvement in customer satisfaction scores compared to pre-training baseline.

Learning Objective Development Worksheet

Step Guiding Questions Your Response
1. Identify the performance need What should learners be able to DO after training?
2. Select the appropriate cognitive level Which level of Bloom's Taxonomy is most appropriate?
3. Choose precise action verbs What specific verb best describes the desired performance?
4. Specify the content/context In what context and with what content will they perform?
5. Determine measurement criteria How will successful achievement be measured?
6. Verify SMART alignment Is the objective Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound?
7. Align with business outcomes How does this objective support desired business results?

Guidelines for Writing Effective Learning Objectives

  1. Start with the end in mind: Begin by identifying what learners should be able to DO after the learning experience.
  2. Use precise action verbs: Choose verbs that clearly describe observable behavior at the appropriate cognitive level.
  3. Focus on learner performance: Frame objectives in terms of what learners will do, not what the instructor will cover.
  4. Incorporate measurable criteria: Include specific conditions and standards for acceptable performance.
  5. Align with assessment strategy: Ensure objectives can be directly measured by planned assessments.
  6. Maintain appropriate scope: Each objective should address a single behavior or outcome.
  7. Connect to business impact: Link objectives to desired organizational results and performance improvements.
  8. Communicate to stakeholders: Write objectives in language understandable to learners, managers, and other stakeholders.
